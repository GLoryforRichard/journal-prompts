## How to Start Journaling in Five Minutes

**To start journaling, open a notebook or notes app, write today's date, and answer one question: “What's on my mind right now?” Write for five minutes without editing, then finish with one small thing you want to do next.** A few sentences count as a complete entry.

You do not need to buy a journal, choose a special method, or commit to writing every day. Try one entry first:

1. **Choose somewhere to write.** Use a notebook, a document, or the free writing area on our [daily journal prompts](/daily-journal-prompts#scene-prompts) page.
2. **Set a five-minute timer.** Give yourself a small, clear stopping point.
3. **Pick one question.** Start with “What happened today, and how did I feel about it?”
4. **Write a few honest sentences.** Include a moment, a feeling, and anything you are still thinking about. Spelling and style do not matter.
5. **Close with a next step.** Finish “Tomorrow, I want to…” and put the journal somewhere easy to find again.

### What a First Journal Entry Looks Like

Here is a fictional example. Yours can be shorter, messier, or about something completely different:

> I felt scattered today because I kept switching between tasks. A short walk after lunch was the one moment I slowed down. Tomorrow, I want to finish one small task before opening my messages.

That is enough. You have described an experience, noticed your response, and chosen something to try. You do not need a dramatic insight or a page full of writing.

## Step 1: Choose Paper or Digital

Pick the format you can reach easily when you want to write.

- **Paper:** A basic notebook works. It can be a good fit if you want time away from a screen or like writing and drawing together.
- **Digital:** A notes app or document works. It can be convenient if you want to type, edit, or keep your journal with you on your phone.
- **A prompt-based journal:** Useful when choosing what to write is the main obstacle. You can answer the prompts on this site in your own notebook or directly in the page.

Think about where your writing is stored. A paper journal needs a suitable place to keep it. For digital writing, check the app's storage and privacy settings before adding anything sensitive.

On this site, guest writing stays in your browser. Clearing browser data removes those entries. A free account lets you save up to 10 entries to your account and access them across devices; wait for the editor to confirm a save before leaving.

## Step 2: Pick a Small, Repeatable Time

Start with two to five minutes at a time that is usually available to you. If writing daily feels like another obligation, choose two or three days a week instead.

**In the morning**, write about what deserves your attention today. Try [morning journal prompts](/morning-journal-prompts) if you like setting an intention before the day starts.

**In the evening**, describe a moment you want to remember or something you are ready to put down for the night.

**During a break**, jot down three sentences about what is on your mind. A journal entry does not have to wait until the house is quiet or the day is finished.

Attach your session to an existing routine: after breakfast, after you close your laptop, or before you pick up a book. Keep the notebook or page easy to open.

## Step 3: Use One Beginner-Friendly Prompt

Choose the question that feels easiest to answer. You do not have to work through every prompt in order.

1. What happened today that I want to remember?
2. How am I feeling right now? What might be contributing to that feeling?
3. What is one thing I appreciated today, and why?
4. What took more energy than I expected?
5. What helped me feel comfortable or supported?
6. What am I looking forward to this week?
7. What is one small thing I can make easier tomorrow?
8. What would I tell a friend who had a day like mine?
9. What did I learn about myself today?
10. What do I need more of right now: rest, company, focus, or something else?

For more options, browse [daily journal prompts](/daily-journal-prompts) or try a specific [gratitude prompt](/gratitude-journal-prompts). Once you have tried a first entry, compare [journaling techniques for beginners](/techniques/for-beginners) to choose a method that fits how you like to write.

## Step 4: Write Without Trying to Make It Good

Treat your journal as a place to think. You can use sentences, bullet points, lists, or a mix of words and sketches. You do not need to explain the whole day.

If you get stuck, use this three-line template:

> Today, I noticed…
>
> I felt… because…
>
> One thing I want to remember or try next is…

Be as specific as you comfortably can. “A friend checked in after my meeting” gives you more to revisit than “People were nice.” You can also write “I don't know yet” and leave a question open.

You decide what belongs in the entry. Skip questions you do not want to answer, and stop if writing feels overwhelming. There is no requirement to explore painful experiences in order to keep a journal.

## Step 5: Finish and Make Returning Easy

Stop when the timer ends if you want to. Add a sentence about what you need next, close the journal, and continue your day.

A missed day does not need a catch-up entry. Write the date you return and start from there. If sessions keep getting skipped, make them smaller: one question and three sentences may fit your life better than a full page.

When you feel like it, look back at a few entries. Notice what you wanted to remember and whether any plans or questions still matter. Your journal does not need a streak or a score to be useful to you.

## A Free Seven-Day Journaling Plan

Use one prompt per session. The days are a suggested sequence, not a deadline.

| Day | What to write | A simple starting sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Describe today | “The moment I keep thinking about is…” |
| 2 | Notice something you appreciate | “One small thing that helped today was…” |
| 3 | Name what you need | “Right now, I could use more…” |
| 4 | Remember a good moment | “I felt most at ease when…” |
| 5 | Make room for a challenge | “Something that feels difficult is…” |
| 6 | Choose a small next step | “One thing I can try is…” |
| 7 | Reflect on the week | “Writing this week helped me notice…” |

## Questions About Starting a Journal

### What should I write in my first journal entry?

Write today's date, one thing that happened, how you felt about it, and a small next step. Three sentences are enough. Use the example at the top of this guide if you need a starting structure.

### How long should a beginner journal?

Try two to five minutes. Continue if you want to, but keep the initial commitment small enough that you can repeat it. There is no required word count.

### Do I have to journal every day?

No. You can write a few times a week or whenever you have something to reflect on. Choose a frequency that supports you, and adjust it when your schedule changes.

### What if I have nothing to write about?

Describe one ordinary moment: a conversation, a meal, a task, or something you noticed outside. Alternatively, finish “Right now, I am thinking about…” and follow that thought for a few sentences.

### Is it better to write by hand or type?

Use whichever you find easier to return to. Handwriting may suit a break from screens; typing may suit writing on the move. You can switch between them without starting your practice over.
